# Statistics Basics

## Measures of center

The mean is the sum of all values divided by their count. The median is the middle value after sorting, and it is not affected by extreme values. The mode is the most frequent value. For skewed data, such as incomes, the median describes a typical value better than the mean.

## Measures of spread

Variance is the average squared distance from the mean. For a sample, the sum of squares is divided by n minus 1 instead of n, which is called Bessel's correction. The standard deviation is the square root of the variance and has the same unit as the data. The interquartile range (IQR) is the difference between the third and the first quartile.

## Outliers

A common rule marks a value as an outlier when it is below Q1 minus 1.5 times IQR or above Q3 plus 1.5 times IQR. Box plots draw exactly these limits as whiskers.

## Normal distribution

In a normal distribution about 68 percent of values lie within one standard deviation of the mean, about 95 percent within two, and about 99.7 percent within three. This is called the empirical rule.

## Correlation

The Pearson correlation coefficient ranges from -1 to 1. A value near 0 means no linear relationship. Correlation does not prove causation.
